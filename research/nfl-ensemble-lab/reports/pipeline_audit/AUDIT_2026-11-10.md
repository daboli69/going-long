Audited commit: e9564c3d8064e91a010bf6aff97dacdba6fd33e9 (branch claude/projections-w10), checkout read on 2026-11-10. Working tree had uncommitted edits to docs/*.json, reports/*/freeze_ledger.jsonl, reports/tracker/*.csv and four lab modules; none of those were changed by this audit. Status labels: VERIFIED means a command run during this audit showed it. LIKELY means strong code reading without execution. SUSPECTED means it needs data that is not available here.

## 1. Verdict

The reported edge is not credible, because there is no edge to report: the lab's own frozen out-of-sample predictions lose to the closing consensus moneyline in every season from 2019 to 2026, the 85-feature six-model champion is indistinguishable from the lab's 3-feature point-differential baseline on the 2025 test season (log loss 0.6354 against 0.6363), and a strict re-run with per-season nested tuning changes these numbers by at most 0.001. The flat-stake tracker (-5.66 units on 32 settled bets) is consistent with the market being right and the model being noise: the expected result under the closing price is -1.4 units, the realized closing line value of the bets is -0.1 probability points, and 32 bets cannot distinguish any plausible ROI from zero (about 6,000 bets are needed for a 5% ROI). The pipeline itself is unusually careful about look-ahead (as-of joins verified by hand, injury rows filtered by timestamp, immutable snapshots with intact hash chain), so the problem is not leakage inflating a result but the absence of any result, plus a bet rule that was fixed after the games it was back-filled on and prices that are a consensus close, not a bettable book.

## 4. The five fixes that matter most

1. Stop treating p_champion as a price signal until a model beats the closing line out of sample. Minimal test: a paired bootstrap of per-game log loss, champion minus market, on 2023 to 2026 walk-forward games; the rule is retired until the 95% interval lies below zero. Today's interval is entirely above zero.
2. Re-register the tracker rule as live-only and discard the backfill from any performance claim. Minimal test: tracker.performance() filtered to origin == "live" must be the only number quoted; evaluations.csv rows with origin backfill are kept for the record but excluded from units.
3. Record a bettable price with each snapshot or label the result as "versus consensus close". Minimal test: for each qualified bet store the best available price among the 10 books already in going_quotes.parquet (h2h market) at snapshot time; a bet counts only if that quote is under 30 minutes old. Report units at that price next to the consensus number.
4. Enforce the FPDS 24-hour availability gate in the weekly command instead of bypassing it. Minimal test: a unit test that calls the weekly entry point with a clock set to 2026-11-10T22:44Z and asserts that week 9 rows are excluded from training and features; rerun Week 10 after 2026-11-11T01:15Z and diff the two forecasts.
5. Split the market benchmark at the 2023 source change and drop invalid odds rows. Minimal test: assert hold between 0.5% and 8% per game before de-vig (one 2020 row fails today), and report model-versus-market metrics for 2016 to 2022 and 2023 onward separately so the 4.3% hold era is not blended with the 2.5% era.

Needs timestamped per-book moneylines at kickoff minus 30 minutes for weeks 6 to 10 (going_quotes.parquet covers h2h but was not joined to the tracker in this audit; a joined table of snapshot time, book, price would settle it).

- Is the 2023 change in games.csv hold a change of book mix or of de-vig convention upstream? Needs nflverse nfldata release notes or a per-book archive for 2022 and 2023.
- Does any FPDS stack beat a matched-window free-NFL control? Needs the pre-registered same-family control run that docs/FPDS_RESEARCH_PROGRAM.md lists as "next" and that has not been run.

## Stage notes

Stage 1 (inventory): feature map written to the audit scratchpad and summarised in section 2 of this file's companion table; the champion uses 85 features in six families (context, opponent-adjusted ratings, projected QB, blended efficiency, last 3 and last 5 form, offense-versus-defense matchups), all built from games with kickoff strictly before the target, plus injury status only through the projected-QB rule.

Stage 2 (leakage): no executed check found target-game information in the game pipeline. The weak points are protocol look-ahead in tuning (negligible, F11), the FPDS gate bypass (F9) and closing lines used as a game environment in one Kalshi arm (F12).

Stage 3 (integrity): schedule, play-by-play and id mapping are clean (F15); the market series changes character in 2023 (F14).

Stage 4 (validation): season-level walk-forward with nested tuning reproduces the reported numbers within 0.001 (section 3).

Stage 5 (calibration and edge): calibration is adequate; the edge is negative in every out-of-sample season (F1, F5, F25).

Stage 6 (overfitting): hundreds of configurations have been evaluated against the same seasons (F7); sample sizes required to show any ROI are in the thousands of bets (F8).

Stage 7 (betting logic): staking is flat and settlement is correct; the rule's threshold and timing (F3, F17), the price (F4) and the unpriced props leans (F18) are the problems.

Stage 8 (reproducibility): training and prediction are reproducible from the feature store (F20, F21); the props pipeline depends on an ignored run folder and non-recoverable local archives (F22).
