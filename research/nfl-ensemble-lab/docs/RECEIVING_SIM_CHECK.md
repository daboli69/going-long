# Sampled receiving model: what was tested

Generated by `scripts/recvsim_check.py`. Every constant was fitted on 2022 to 2024 games; everything below is scored on 2025 and 2026 games the fit never saw, using only information from before each game. Aggregate numbers only.

The model draws a team's targets for the game, splits them among its players with a Dirichlet-multinomial so each game comes out lumpy, then draws catches and yards. Fitted: k 50, prior 0.02, w_fpds 0, eps 0.02, alpha 36, alpha_short 24, alpha_mult 1.25, r_nb 43.1, t_slope 0.445, t_icpt 17.6, m_c 80, m_y 32, sigma_c 0.151. FantasyPoints target share got weight 0.00: raw play-by-play targets already measure the same thing.

## c. Against the market's prices

Archived Kalshi prices exist only for weeks [8] of 2026, and no game with prices has been played yet, so the model cannot be scored against outcomes. This step will score itself on the first Tuesday after those games finish.

For now, closeness only: across 183 receiving thresholds the sampled model sits 0.070 from the market price on average (probability points), against 0.059 for the lab's earlier simulation. Being close is not the same as being right.

The sampled model moves no headline number: the winner, spread, total and props tables still come from the market and the saved projections. It only draws the replay.

## d. Can a classifier tell real from simulated?

A gradient-boosted classifier was trained to tell real team receiving box scores from simulated ones (630 team-games each, five-fold cross-validation). Area under the curve: 0.5 means it cannot tell, 1.0 means it always can.

- Real versus the sampled model: 0.56
- Real versus the old averaged method: 0.87

A blind sheet of 12 real and 12 simulated box scores is in `reports/blind_boxscores.md` (answers in `reports/blind_boxscores_key.json`) for a person to try.
