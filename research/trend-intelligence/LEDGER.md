## E1: Prior-season role profile -> next-season receiving volume and yards (WR/TE) - REJECTED

*Pre-registered 2026-10-08T01:47:04Z (spec `13db4ff5c45c`).*

- **What we thought:** A fifteen-feature prior-season role profile (routes, TPRR, first-read share, alignment, XFP) would improve next-season targets, receptions and yards per game over the player's own prior production.
- **Why football supports it:** Targets follow routes run, how often a player is the first read and where he lines up; production-only baselines mix role with luck and with a one-off team context. Role is closer to the cause of volume.
- **How tested without leakage:** features from season N-1 only; season N file used as outcome only; chronological rolling origin; baselines {"B0": "prior-season per-game value of the same outcome", "B1": "ridge on prior per-game targets, receptions, yards, games played, TE flag"}
- **Result:** REJECTED by the registered rule. The challenger was 3% WORSE than the baseline model on all three outcomes (better in only 1 of 3 test seasons). It lost in the first two test seasons, when it was trained on about 100 rows, and won in 2025 after more training data, the signature of an over-parameterised model rather than evidence that role carries no information.
- **What GOING should do:** Do not use the fifteen-feature profile. The compact version E6 is exploratory and cannot support promotion; confirm it on 2026 weekly data.

## E3: Weekly application: do prior-season role features add to what GOING already knows in-season (rolling usage)? - INCONCLUSIVE

*Pre-registered 2026-10-08T01:47:04Z (spec `af0fdbe3977d`).*

- **What we thought:** Prior-season role features add to what GOING already knows in-season (rolling usage), most in weeks 1-4.
- **Why football supports it:** Early in a season rolling usage is a handful of games; role history is a better estimate of the true role. Later the sample dominates.
- **How tested without leakage:** prior-season features only; rolling means use weeks strictly before the target week; chronological rolling origin; baselines {"B1": "ridge on prior-season per-game targets/receptions/yards (nflverse weekly) + TE flag", "B2": "B1 + mean of the player's earlier weeks this season (prior average if none), number of earlier appearances"}
- **Result:** Not supported. In weeks 1-4 the challenger was 1.0% worse (yards) and 0.4% worse (receptions) and 0.3% better (targets, inconclusive) than the baseline that already contains prior averages and rolling in-season usage. Later buckets were consistently worse. Separately, in-season rolling usage clearly matters: it lowers MAE by 3.3-5.0% in weeks 1-4 and 5-12% later versus prior-season averages alone.
- **What GOING should do:** Do not add Fantasy Points role features to weekly volume projections on this evidence. Make sure GOING's early-season props blend prior-season averages with in-season usage (large measured gain).
