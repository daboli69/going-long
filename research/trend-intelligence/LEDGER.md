## E7: Dynamic blend of prior and current-season usage vs the production 80/20 policy - PROMISING

*Pre-registered 2026-10-08T10:47:03Z (spec `f466075db1c0`).*

- **What we thought:** The production 80% current-season / 20% earlier-season weighting is too aggressive in the first weeks; a blend whose prior counts as c pseudo-games predicts the weekly mean better.
- **Why football supports it:** One or two games are a noisy read of a role; early in a season the earlier seasons contain most of the real information, and by midseason the current role dominates. The 80/20 rule jumps to 80% after a single game.
- **How tested without leakage:** window = games strictly before the target week; chronological rolling origin; comparators {"W0": "Champion (production 80/20)", "W1": "equal weights over the window (no season weighting)", "W3": "learned shrinkage: (k*current_mean + c*prior_mean)/(k+c), c from the grid [0.5..40] chosen on TRAIN seasons only per market", "W4": "ridge on current mean, prior mean, k, k=0 flag, prior games"}
- **Result:** PROMISING (historical, mean accuracy by MAE) in 4 of 8 markets, weeks 1-6 vs the reconstructed Champion: QB passing yards -6.7% MAE and QB passing TDs -6.2% (3/3 seasons; equal-weight windows do as well, c about 3-4), receptions -1.2% and receiving yards -1.3% (3/3 seasons, c about 1-2; the gain comes almost entirely from players with exactly one current-season game, where the Champion puts 80% of the weight on a single game). All-weeks gains are smaller (QB 2.3-2.5%; receiving 0.4-0.5%, REJECTED under the registered v2 rule). Touchdown markets and rushing yards showed no MAE difference, but MAE is the wrong loss for 0/1-like outcomes (QA): see E7b, which uses the Poisson log score. The repo's 2026 season review reports a similar direction for equal vs 80/20 weights; that is a separate, unaudited analysis and is not independent confirmation.
- **What GOING should do:** Strongest evidence so far that the Champion can be improved, and it is about WEIGHTING, not Fantasy Points. Candidate Challenger: replace 0.8/0.2 with k/(k+c) shrinkage. Not promoted: it improves the projected mean, not demonstrated on market prices (no historical prop lines), and the 2026 prospective check (E10) is too small to say.

- **Why football supports it:** Same as E7. For 0/1-like outcomes the mean absolute error is a poor loss; the log score is the proper score for the Poisson model that prices them.
- **How tested without leakage:** as E7; chronological rolling origin; comparators {"W0": "Champion (production 80/20)", "W3": "learned shrinkage with c chosen on TRAIN seasons by mean Poisson log score"}
- **Result:** All five count markets cleared the pre-registered bar in 3 of 3 test seasons (mean log-score gain per row: receptions +0.062, receiving TD +0.035, rushing TD +0.038, anytime TD +0.044, QB passing TD +0.092 nats; adjusted CIs exclude 0; chosen c = 2 for receptions, 3-4 for the rest). Caveat: the log score floors a predicted mean at 0.01, so part of the gain comes from players whose Champion mean is at or near zero (a zero Poisson mean gives a huge penalty when the event happens); how production prices a zero mean was not audited here, so some of this gain may not exist in the live probabilities. - **What GOING should do:** Challenger idea: shrink toward the prior-season mean for count markets and never let a mean reach zero. Verify how production handles zero means, then test on 2026 prices before anything is promoted.
